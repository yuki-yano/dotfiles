# Casting the reader

A review needs a reader, and "the audience" is not one. Cast two personas
per review: one sympathetic, one skeptical. Both are honest; they differ in
what they want and what stopping costs them (nothing, ever).

Every cast states four things before the read begins:

1. **Priors**: what they already know and believe about this topic, and what
   they have already read this week that this piece competes with.
2. **Situation**: where they meet the text (feed on a phone, inbox, a link a
   colleague sent, a docs search result) and what they were doing before.
3. **Patience budget**: how much they give the opening before bailing, from
   the empirical table below.
4. **Stake**: why they might care, in one sentence. If you cannot write this
   sentence, that is the first finding of the review.

## Patience budgets (empirical, per encounter context)

| Context | Budget for the opening | Source basis |
|---|---|---|
| Social feed scroll | 10 to 15 seconds, roughly the first two lines | Chartbeat: ~55% of pageviews under 15s engaged time |
| Blog/newsletter via link | first screen, ~2 paragraphs | NN/g: at most ~20 to 28% of words read; commitment earned by the opening |
| Inbox (memo, cover letter) | under 30 seconds | recruiter/hiring-manager survey data; Ladders eye-tracking 7.4s initial resume scan |
| Slush pile / gatekeeper | first paragraph, one strike allowed | slush-reader practice: most rejections happen on page one |
| Investor / exec skim | ~20 seconds per section, fixates on team/numbers/ask | DocSend: 3m44s average per deck |
| Docs / reference lookup | as long as scanning WORKS; zero tolerance for prose blocking the answer | information foraging: reader follows scent to the answer |
| Committed subscriber / fan | full read, but skimming the middle is still on the table | median scroll depth ~50% even for engaged visits |

## The two default casts

**The sympathetic reader** wants the piece to be good. They followed a
recommendation, they like the topic, they read generously and finish unless
actively pushed out. Their transcript shows what the piece is at its best
(Elbow's believing game). If even this reader skims a section, that section
is dead.

**The skeptical gatekeeper** is the genre's professional stopper: slush
reader, staff engineer skimming a design doc, editor, investor, tired
customer. They are looking for a reason to stop, one is enough, and an early
credibility hit taxes everything after it. Their quit point is the review's
sharpest number (the doubting game).

Adjust both to the piece's real audience. A skill README's skeptic is a
developer deciding in 30 seconds whether to install; a personal essay's
skeptic is a stranger who has read forty essays this week that open the
same way.

## Casting rules

- Personas must disagree with each other somewhere or the cast failed;
  rerun the skeptic with a tighter budget and a sharper stake.
- Never cast the author, and never cast "someone who loves this style".
- The persona's knowledge sets what needs explaining: an expert reader
  experiences a definition of basics as being talked down to; a newcomer
  experiences undefined jargon as a locked door. Both are needle events.
- If the user cannot say who the reader is, cast the skeptic from the venue
  (where will this be published?) and say in the report that the cast is a
  guess.
